02  —  Efficient Frontier

Optimal Risk–Return Trade-off

The efficient frontier traces the minimum-variance portfolio for every achievable return. Portfolios below the frontier are sub-optimal. The max-Sharpe tangency portfolio maximises return per unit of risk.

Efficient Frontier — Equity + Bonds + Infrastructure
Pension-constrained (solid) vs. unconstrained long-only (dashed). Hover for portfolio weights.
Unconstrained max-Sharpe (Infra): 7.3% Equity / 0% Bonds / 92.7% Infrastructure — Sharpe 0.969. Academically optimal but operationally impossible for a pension fund. Infrastructure's superior risk-adjusted return vs equity means MVO loads heavily into it.

Pension-constrained max-Sharpe: 30% Equity / 40% Bonds / 30% Infrastructure — Sharpe 0.894. This is the best achievable within institutional bounds. It still represents a significant shift from the 60/40 norm.
Efficient Frontier — Equity + Bonds + Private Credit
Pension-constrained (solid) vs. unconstrained long-only (dashed). Hover for portfolio weights.
Unconstrained max-Sharpe (PC): 23.2% Equity / 34.7% Bonds / 42.1% Private Credit — Sharpe 0.819. Private Credit's very low volatility (3.6%) and low correlation to bonds makes it attractive as a bond substitute in MVO.

Pension-constrained max-Sharpe: 30.6% Equity / 44.4% Bonds / 25% Private Credit — Sharpe 0.815. Much of the MVO benefit comes from reducing equity exposure rather than from Private Credit's standalone return.

06  —  Conclusions

Key Findings for the Investment Committee

Optimal Infra Portfolio
50% Eq / 20% Bd / 30% Infra
Return 10.2% · Vol 10.4% · Sharpe 0.856
Pension-practical, high equity retention, max Sharpe within 30% alt cap
Optimal PC Portfolio
50% Eq / 30% Bd / 20% PC
Return 8.7% · Vol 9.2% · Sharpe 0.797
Lower vol, but Sharpe improvement over 60/40 is minimal (+0.005)
Finding 1 — Infrastructure warrants a larger-than-10% allocation. Across all equity levels tested (40–60%), the Sharpe-optimal infrastructure weight is the maximum allowed (30% cap). Every additional 5% infrastructure replacing bonds improves Sharpe by approximately 6–8 bps. The prior fixed 10% allocation is clearly sub-optimal.
Finding 2 — Private Credit's Sharpe gains are modest and equity-level-dependent. With equity fixed at 60%, adding Private Credit actually reduces Sharpe (bonds at 40% already dominate the low-vol role). PC only improves Sharpe when equity is reduced to 45–50%, because it then substitutes for both bonds and some equity risk. It is a bond replacement, not an equity diversifier.
Finding 3 — MVO pushes toward dramatic equity reduction that pension funds won't accept. The unconstrained optimizer recommends 7% equity (Infrastructure) or 23% equity (Private Credit) — far outside pension fund norms. The pension-constrained solution (30% equity floor) is the operative result, but even this requires board approval for a significant equity underweight relative to typical 55–65% strategic ranges. Practical recommendation: use a 50% equity anchor with 20–30% infrastructure.
Finding 4 — The optimal portfolio depends on the objective. If maximising Sharpe: 30/40/30 (Infra) is optimal. If maintaining returns near 10%: 50/20/30 (Infra). If minimising drawdown risk: 55/30/15 (Infra) is a reasonable compromise. There is no single "optimal" — the committee must specify whether Sharpe, return, or drawdown is the primary objective.
